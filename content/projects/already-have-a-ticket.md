sections:
  - heading: Problem
    text: "A ticket confirmation tells you what you bought. It leaves out the one
      thing you need on arrival: if you bought online, you can go straight to
      the usher. So on busy nights people with prepaid tickets queued at the box
      office just to ask where to go. The cinema has no app; this prototype is
      my own unofficial model of it."
  - heading: Prototype
    image: images/already-have-a-ticket_section-prototype_1200x900.jpg
    alt: A paper sketch of three phone screens, with an “Admission” button on the
      ticket confirmation.
    text: "I started on paper, then built three linked phone screens in Figma: the
      evening's listings, a ticket confirmation and an arrival guide. I wrote
      the copy and linked the screens myself. My first confirmation hid the
      instruction behind a button labelled “Admission”: the word we use behind
      the counter, not the word a visitor looks for."
  - heading: Research
    image: images/already-have-a-ticket_section-research_900x1200.jpg
    alt: A handwritten tally sheet recording where three testers tapped on the
      confirmation screen.
    text: I handed three classmates who had never worked the door the confirmation
      screen and said, “You've bought your ticket. You're outside and there's a
      line at the window. Show me what you'd do.” A classmate took notes while I
      watched. Two went back to the listings. The third found “Admission” and
      asked whether she was about to buy another ticket. In the first session I
      caught myself pointing at the button.
  - heading: Iteration
    image: images/already-have-a-ticket_section-iteration_1200x800.jpg
    alt: Two phone confirmation screens side by side; the left has an “Admission”
      button, the right a sentence directing ticket holders to the blue doors.
    text: >-
      I moved one sentence onto the confirmation itself: “Already have a ticket?
      Go straight to the usher inside the blue doors.” Then I drew in the blue
      doors, because that's the detail I'd point to from the booth.


      Left: the first confirmation sends you looking for “Admission.” Right: the
      revised confirmation tells a prepaid visitor which line to skip and which
      doors to use.


      My shortest draft was “Skip the line.” I crossed it out. On a busy night
      there are two lines, and somebody with a prepaid ticket still has to wait
      for the usher.
  - heading: Reflection
    text: "The revision hasn't been tested yet, and the flow stops before payment.
      My classmates could show me where my wording confused them; they can't
      tell me what real customers would do. Next I want to try it with someone
      who has never seen the building: would they know which doors I mean?"

On sold-out nights at the cinema where I work summers, people who had already bought tickets online kept waiting at my window to ask where to go. For a class project I sketched a phone guide that answers that question before they reach the glass, then watched three classmates try it.
